# Research behind complete learning in Cauchy

## Product standard

The goal is to help someone learn the supplied subject thoroughly and demonstrate their reasoning independently. Study speed is a secondary measure. A fast lesson that omits a proof, an essential hypothesis or a prerequisite has not met the goal. An eight-hour target must be evaluated against a defined syllabus, starting knowledge, independent assessment and delayed retention; it is not a product guarantee.

This review draws on primary learning-science research, a government evidence review, proof-education research and recent empirical AI-tutoring studies. Evidence from introductory physics or high-school mathematics is useful, but does not establish effectiveness for an entire university proof-based course. The design decisions below are our inferences from that evidence, not findings about Cauchy itself.

## Evidence and its limits

| Source | Finding relevant to Cauchy | Limits and implementation decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [National Academies, *How People Learn II*, chapter 5 (2018)](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/7) | Prior knowledge supports new learning; explaining material can help learners organize knowledge, but weak prior knowledge can make self-explanation ineffective. Strategy effectiveness depends on learner, material and goals. | A consensus synthesis, not a Cauchy trial. Show prerequisites, use a diagnostic, and expose missing source context instead of forcing the learner to explain something they have not been equipped to understand. |
| [National Academies, *How Students Learn*, introduction (2005)](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10126/chapter/2) | Competence involves factual knowledge organized within a conceptual framework that supports retrieval and application. | Consensus synthesis, not a trial of this course builder. A whole-document path must relate topics and their foundations; isolated local exercises alone do not meet that design goal. Whole-course planning and bounded teaching steps serve different purposes and both need evaluation. |
| [IES, *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning* (2007)](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1), [full guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf) | Recommends spacing, alternating worked examples and problems, connecting concrete and abstract representations, retrieval and deep explanatory questions. Evidence strength differs by recommendation. | Strong evidence is assigned to re-exposure through quizzes and deep explanatory questions; several other recommendations are moderate or minimal. Keep worked reasoning, learner attempts and independent checks; do not equate one immediate check with retention. |
| [Chi et al., *Self-Explanations* (1989)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1) | Examines learners' explanations while studying worked mechanics examples and their subsequent problem solving. Productive explanations connect steps to underlying principles. | Abstract reviewed; this is not a claim that every self-explanation prompt works equally well. Ask why a step follows, and teach enough of the underlying principle to make the question answerable. |
| [Karpicke & Blunt, *Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning…* (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) | Retrieval practice improved conceptual learning of science texts, including comprehension and inference questions, relative to the study conditions examined. | Abstract reviewed. Science-text retrieval is not proof-construction training. Retain independent retrieval, while requiring arguments and applications separately rather than using factual recall to stand in for either. |
| [Mejía-Ramos et al., *An assessment model for proof comprehension* (2012)](https://www.researchwithrutgers.org/en/publications/an-assessment-model-for-proof-comprehension-in-undergraduate-math/) | Proof comprehension includes statement meanings, logical status and chaining, high-level ideas, modules, methods and connections with examples. | Peer-reviewed conceptual assessment model; abstract reviewed, not a measured effect of this interface. Teach precise statements, assumptions, strategy, justified steps and conclusion; assess reasoning rather than recognition of a theorem. |
| [Fuller et al., *Comprehending structured proofs* (2014)](https://jieem.pgsskroton.com.br/article/download/84/74) | Structured presentation helped students identify a proof summary, but students sometimes found the navigation disjointed. Other comprehension measures were often worse, usually without statistical significance. | Results do not justify claiming a universal benefit from a proof tree. Keep a continuous argument with each justification beside its claim; an overview supplements the chain rather than replacing it. |
| [Rey et al., *A Meta-analysis of the Segmenting Effect* (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4) | Across 56 multimedia-learning investigations, segmentation was associated with small to medium benefits for retention and transfer, less cognitive load, and more learning time. | Publisher abstract reviewed. This does not establish a benefit for paginated mathematical proofs or faster learning. Cauchy's paced proof is a design hypothesis: retain each full claim and justification, keep previous-step and global context available, and offer the continuous argument without forcing sequential clicks. |
| [Bastani et al., *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), [author manuscript](https://hamsabastani.github.io/education_llm.pdf) | In a field experiment involving nearly a thousand high-school mathematics students, AI improved supported practice performance. The unrestricted interface reduced subsequent unassisted exam performance; tutor safeguards largely mitigated that harm without establishing an exam improvement over control. | Specific school, materials and GPT-4-based interventions; the manuscript explicitly distinguishes short-term exams from long-term learning. Preserve learner effort, targeted hints and unassisted assessment. Completion time or assisted answer accuracy cannot be the primary outcome. |
| [Kestin et al., *AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning* (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6) | An RCT with 194 eligible Harvard physics students found higher post-test learning with a custom pedagogically designed tutor on two lessons. | Short-term, two-topic evidence in a specific course; it does not demonstrate whole-course completeness or lasting proof ability. The encouraging result supports careful instructional design and actual outcome evaluation, not a blanket speed claim. |
| [*Generative AI alone may not be enough: Learning Mathematical Proof* (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16778v1) | The reported proof-course study found an improvement in homework during the initial intervention comparison but no statistically significant group differences on exams. | Preprint; later usage correlations are not causal evidence of benefit or harm. Proof learning needs separate independent measures; a polished chat experience and better homework are insufficient. |
| [Google DeepMind et al., *Towards Responsible Development…: An Evaluation-Driven Approach* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12687v3) | Describes diverse pedagogical benchmarks, expert review and human evaluation alongside automated critics. Notes limitations of model critics and of single-turn evaluation. | Model-development research, not evidence that Cauchy works. Use reproducible fixtures and expert-reviewed answer keys, then evaluate complete learning interactions and delayed outcomes. An LLM judging another LLM is not the final quality standard. |

## Changes implemented from this review

The [17 October reconciliation audit](evaluation/source-reconciliation.md) adds a practical boundary between source accounting and source adequacy. It uses full-document text search, bounded page retrieval and reference-following to investigate local-excerpt warnings. Literal page evidence and transparent search scope are required; a search miss cannot establish omission, and model citation correctness does not establish mathematical validity. The original obligations and independent learning evidence remain separate.

The study map inventories all supplied PDF pages in bounded sections. Each page has an explicit disposition. Source quotes and page references are validated; unreadable or oversized text is not silently discarded. Neighbouring pages provide context when they fit. Incomplete arguments remain explicit gaps. Successfully processed sections are saved so cancellation and failure do not erase earlier work.

Each mapped unit has separate concept, proof where relevant, and application requirements. Checks attach to the specific requirement. Passing an application does not satisfy the proof requirement. Missing source proofs remain open even when the other checks pass. The map reports source accounting and assessment evidence separately, without a whole-course mastery percentage.

Proof teaching includes the exact statement, assumptions, strategy, every claim and justification, and the conclusion. The guided view presents one part at a time; the complete continuous argument remains available alongside it. Back, direct part selection, and direct entry to practice avoid forcing needless clicks. The learner's place is saved, but browsing never creates assessment evidence. The old 100-word teaching and 70-word answer-key instructions have been removed. Proof questions carry explicit grading criteria. Assessments must address every criterion; a reported missing essential criterion prevents a correct grade, and uncertainty remains uncertainty. Correct alternatives are allowed by the grading instructions.

The map recommends resuming an open requirement, otherwise the next unchecked requirement in the prepared course order. It does not measure prerequisite mastery. Topics expand on demand, with source-gap counts visible when collapsed. Feedback leads with the assessment and next action; the complete criterion review remains available through a disclosure. These interface choices follow the content-and-controls hierarchy in [Apple's Materials HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), keeping Liquid Glass on functional controls and readable content on the content layer.

The default entry now prepares a path for the entire PDF, without asking the learner to choose page ranges. Detailed page accounting is followed by whole-document organization: modules, a foundation-first order and explicit connections between topics. Larger inventories use regional organization followed by global composition. Every identified unit must survive exactly once, and the global order cannot reverse retained regional dependencies. The detailed source inventory, proof obligations and gaps are preserved separately. These checks establish structural integrity, not that the generated connections are mathematically correct or that the inventory is exhaustive. Lessons receive their module goal and related connections as orientation, while mathematical claims still require the supplied source.

The one-passage tool remains an explicit secondary route. This applies [Apple's onboarding guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding): the core workflow should be understandable through use, with optional details presented where they are needed. The learner-facing sequence is open notes → prepare the whole path → study and practise → continue within the path. Preparation progress reports document reading and connecting ideas, rather than exposing model batches as the learner's unit of study.

These are structural and workflow improvements. Source quotes do not prove an argument is valid, and a model can misidentify material, generate an invalid example, or incorrectly mark a criterion satisfied. Cauchy does not yet certify educational completeness.

## Evaluation before calling this production quality

Use expert-reviewed notes and independent answer keys across linear algebra, analysis, probability and at least one non-mathematical subject. Include clean PDFs, poor extraction, cross-page proofs, omitted proofs, diagrams with essential information, missing prerequisites and a syllabus that contains material absent from the notes.

Measure coverage omissions per required syllabus item, not only pages processed. Audit every theorem's hypotheses and proof obligation. Assess definitions, local proof justification, global proof strategy, proof construction and application separately. Test invalid proofs with correct conclusions, circular reasoning, examples offered as universal proofs, missing cases, wrong quantifiers, and valid alternative arguments. Report false acceptance and false rejection separately, including uncertainty and answer-key errors.

Use blinded expert ratings of complete tutoring interactions as well as automated regression checks. Evaluate unassisted transfer immediately and after a delay, with comparable difficulty and alternate questions. Time saved, satisfaction and assisted performance are secondary. A pre/post comparison alone cannot establish that Cauchy caused the gain; a credible controlled comparison is needed for efficacy claims.

The [chapter-transfer pass](evaluation/chapter-challenge.md) now implements a bounded prerequisite refresher within a cumulative two-idea argument, preserving the original draft and recording help. This is an implementation of the design hypothesis, not evidence of improved learning. The remaining product work is syllabus reconciliation, broader prerequisite remediation, human correction of the generated inventory, and a delayed-review workflow. These should preserve proof obligations and source gaps rather than turn the study map into a speed-oriented checklist.
